## Fixed Style Rules

Use case: stylized-concept

Primary request:
Create one full-body table-tennis player character as a clean cutout. The character must look like a collectible arcade sports-game figure, similar to a Korean baseball game character card style: cute, polished, stylized, and readable, but still adult.

Style:
- 3D sports-game caricature, not photorealistic and not semi-realistic.
- Consistent 3-head body ratio: head is large but not huge, body is compact and athletic, hands and shoes are slightly emphasized but natural.
- Smooth toy/figure material with simplified facial planes.
- No realistic skin pores, no raw photo face, no face pasted onto a cartoon body.
- Adult expression. Do not make the player look like a baby, toddler, child, or generic cute boy.

Card cutout rules:

- Transparent or clean white background suitable for alpha cutout.
- No card frame, no rank badge, no text, no watermark.
- Full body visible, including both shoes.
- Keep feet inside the canvas with enough margin for card composition.

Sportswear:
- Modern round-neck table-tennis uniform.
- Athletic fabric with believable paneling, seams, subtle texture, and color variation.
- Not a polo shirt.
- Not mostly black.
- No readable brand logos or text.

Table-tennis correctness:
- The racket grip must match the player's registered grip.
- Handedness must match the player's registered hand.
- Racket hand must be clearly visible.
- For a front-facing shakehand racket: show the red forehand rubber face toward the viewer, but the hand must grip high at the blade neck, directly under the blade shoulder.
- For shakehand grip: the thumb is visible on the front side near the blade/handle shoulder. The thumb presses/supports the front side, short and natural.
- For shakehand grip: the index finger goes behind the racket to the backhand side. It must not lie across the red front face. Its path behind the blade may be implied by a small side/edge knuckle or fingertip behind the handle shoulder, but no long front-side index finger is visible.
- For shakehand grip: the middle finger, ring finger, little finger, and palm hold the handle high and close to the blade neck. These three lower fingers wrap the handle separately and visibly, not as one clenched fist.
- For shakehand grip: the wooden handle remains partly visible between and below the fingers, but the grip is high, not low on the bottom of the handle.
- The hand must look physically possible and relaxed, with clear gaps/creases between the three handle fingers.
- No malformed fingers, no extra fingers, no fused fingers.
- Do not add a table, net, ball, arena background, or opponent for the cutout.
